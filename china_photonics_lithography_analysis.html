Executive thesis

China is strong downstream in modules and communications hardware, but weaker upstream in the precision tools, materials, and photonic/electronic integration needed for the next generation of systems.

Bottom line: The gap is less about a lack of research creativity and more about converting demonstrations and pilot capability into precision, yield, reliability, qualified supply and repeatable volume economics.

9. Optical computing: strong demonstrations, weaker general-purpose proof

Chinese universities have produced impressive optical-computing demonstrations, including Tsinghua’s ACCEL work. But CSIS cautioned that these systems were narrow, task-specific, and not yet equivalent to general-purpose GPUs.[12]

The key questions for any impressive conference demo are:

Workload
What workload?
Precision
What precision?
Architecture
Analog or digital?
Mode
Inference only or training too?
Electronic overhead
Does the optical result require electronic correction?
Total energy
Include lasers, ADCs, DACs, memory and control.
Programmability
Is it programmable?
Generality
Can it run a broad model family?
Commercial reality
Has it shipped beyond the lab?

On this front, China is not necessarily behind in research creativity. It is behind in converting many of those demonstrations into robust, programmable, economically viable products.

The “West” is not one team

The leading Western/allied stack is distributed:

Europe

  • Netherlands: ASML lithography-system integration
  • Germany: ZEISS EUV optics; TRUMPF high-power laser systems
  • Belgium: imec process integration, photonic foundry, packaging and reliability research

US, Japan and Taiwan

  • United States: Cymer heritage in EUV sources, KLA metrology, Applied Materials/Lam equipment, EDA, optical I/O and photonics startups
  • Japan: DUV systems, photoresists, mask and materials ecosystem
  • Taiwan: TSMC leading-edge process integration and yield learning

That distributed ecosystem is itself a moat. China is trying to recreate it inside one national supply chain.

My bottom-line assessment

China is clearly behind in

  1. Commercial EUV lithography
  2. High-NA EUV
  3. EUV source power and efficiency
  4. Ultra-precision lithography optics
  5. Leading-edge photoresist and mask materials
  6. Lithography metrology and inspection at the frontier
  7. Scanner field reliability and service infrastructure
  8. Photonic/electronic co-packaging at hyperscale
  9. Mature design-kit, IP and customer-qualification ecosystems

China is competitive or strong in

  1. Optical transceiver manufacturing
  2. Fiber-optic communications
  3. Telecom infrastructure
  4. Industrial lasers
  5. Mature-node photonic fabrication
  6. Applied optical components
  7. Fast domestic deployment driven by state procurement
  8. Academic photonic computing and quantum-photonics research

The test to apply to conference teams

For every team claiming a breakthrough, ask for:

  1. Exact process node and wafer size
  2. Scanner model and light source
  3. Resolution, overlay, throughput and uptime
  4. Wafer yield and defect density
  5. Number of wafers processed
  6. Packaging loss and fiber-coupling loss
  7. Thermal cycling and reliability data
  8. Customer shipment or qualification evidence
  9. Whether the result is a paper, prototype, pilot line, sample or volume product

The Chinese conference landscape may look full of advanced photonics because China has a lot of research, pilots, component production and state-backed commercialization. That is real progress. The remaining gap is the brutal industrial layer: precision, yield, reliability, materials and repeatable volume economics.
